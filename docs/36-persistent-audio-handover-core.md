# Persistent audio while replacing video: transport and scheduler foundation

Date: 2026-10-22, Europe/Budapest. This increment prepares quality changes that preserve one audio subscription, scheduler and device. It does **not** connect that handover to the Qt viewer yet. Normal UI receiving remains video-only; the controlled A/V viewer still stops/replaces its complete watch on a quality change. Audible network-to-output continuity through a UI switch is therefore not verified by this increment.

## Change

Previously a watch selected video and optional audio together. Replacing the watch revoked both tracks, joined the audio worker and recreated playback. The transport now additionally supports immutable audio-only and video-only watches, exact-object retirement, and independent verified-track readiness. Existing combined watches retain their paired readiness and sticky revoke behavior. Audio-only watches cannot grant video presentation.

The room refuses duplicate subscriptions to any SID, including a retiring watch. A return to a previous quality must wait for its old watch to stop and create a fresh object. Watch bookkeeping is bounded by three distinct owner/share selections and nine role watches in total, including retiring objects. This permits a persistent audio subscription, current video and temporary candidate for each planned share; it does not establish three-share performance. Whole-share cancellation, room invalidation and generation retirement still close every matching role.

`RemoteMediaSelection` pairs one fixed audio lease with an active video lease and at most one candidate. Room admission must be the same object, and authenticated owner, share ID and generation must match. Audio must be an audio-only lease; both video leases must be video-only, with different qualities and SIDs. A lease for an identical-looking share in another room cannot be combined. The owner still controls actual reader/subscription lifetime.

Final operations take leases in one order: audio, active video, then candidate if that candidate is being painted/adopted. Audio and current-video operations do not require candidate readiness. An unverified or failed candidate cannot disrupt the verified current selection. Revoking audio or the active video prevents subsequent endpoint commits; revoking any of the three prevents candidate output/adoption. An operation already admitted under those locks may finish before revoke returns, as in the existing bounded device-commit contract. Selection cancellation does not itself stop an SDK reader; its owner must retire that watch separately.

Candidate timing is projected onto the established common clock without consuming PCM, moving the anchor or changing reset serial. Projection requires valid native RTCP metadata, an established media commit, a fresh active video clock, and 10–80 ms of candidate deadline headroom. Bad candidate metadata, priming and wrong dimensions are dropped without resetting active playback. RTCP timing remains infrastructure supplied and is never encryption permission.

Adoption is an explicit transaction intended for the future actual-frame-queued callback. It rechecks every required lease, the exact staged object and attempt, the unchanged timeline serial/mapping, native clock validity, original arrival age, active-video freshness and a due presentation no more than 80 ms late. The new video SSRC has its own epoch; it must not be compared to the retired track's numeric epoch. Successful adoption updates video freshness/presentation while retaining audio ordering, PCM, the common mapping and the endpoint. Subsequent ingress or painting from the old object is rejected. A cancelled attempt or timing reset invalidates its candidate tickets.

The PCM ring is still six 10 ms blocks, with 60 ms residence. The core remains 11,928 bytes: the new core functions add no state. Selection bookkeeping and two diagnostic counters add adapter state, with no additional PCM or image storage. This increment does not allocate a second video image queue because the GUI has not been connected yet; that future overlap must remain bounded and have an explicit timeout/rollback.

## Verification

The new `persistent_audio_video_handover` native test uses a fake endpoint and verified synthetic leases. It checks exact sample values and totals through 720p → 1080p → 720p, unchanged device-reset count and serial, independently numbered video epochs, continued audio during warm-up, rejection of retired callbacks and a fresh return lease. It also tests candidate cancellation/restaging, timing resets, unverified/failed candidates, owner/share/generation/room mismatches, invalid clocks/dimensions/priming, stale candidates and extreme deadlines.

Concurrency controls revoke each required lease during a candidate paint and revoke each active media role during a device commit. Revoke must wait for an earlier admitted operation, and later output must fail. Separate controls revoke between reservation and commit and invalidate room admission. Existing native regressions continue to cover endpoint failures, render stalls, established-playback jitter, final admission and queue limits.

Two local encrypted transport cases inspect independent audio/video subscriptions, temporary dual-quality decoding, exact retirement, a fresh return to 720p, generation unpublish and wrong-key rejection. They use generated media and no screen/audio capture or playback. Continued audio callbacks and the same immutable audio watch demonstrate subscription lifetime; they do not prove no dropped packets, audible continuity, final presentation or physical synchronization. Existing combined-watch and variable-frame-rate cases remain separate regressions.

All 22 native tests pass. All four encrypted transport cases pass, including the split subscription, wrong key and existing combined-watch/variable-rate regressions. In the ten-case current-build A/V matrix, nine pass; the paint-stall case fails its `paint_stall_exercised` check despite confirming that the 220 ms stall was performed. It records no presentation-age reset, one endpoint-gap reset and 98.20% media-window coverage. No assertion was weakened.

Two targeted repeats on that same current binary also fail. The first does exercise the presentation-age reset but drops to **78.00% media-window coverage**, with 114,741 silent output frames and 120,063 late frames. The trace-enabled repeat again misses the required presentation-reset counter, with 98.29% coverage and two endpoint-gap resets. This is an unresolved playback/recovery failure, not a handover success. A separate control using the hash-verified preceding frozen app also reproduces the missing presentation-counter check, with 98.20% coverage and an endpoint-gap reset. That control demonstrates the counter-path issue predates this foundation; it does not establish the cause or history of the current build's 78% coverage drop.

The bounded trace retains the last 128 events near the end of the run; it cannot establish the exact earlier stall transition. No hardware fault-recovery success or root cause is claimed. The diagnostic comparison is retained in [the stall record](../artifacts/handover-core-stall-diagnostics.json), with every original report and failed check intact. All five standalone WASAPI cases and all six normal video-only GUI cases pass on the current binaries, including the existing video-only quality switch. Historical snapshots remain unchanged.

The primary regression matrices therefore pass **24 of 25 cases**, alongside all **22 native tests**. Two further current-build diagnostic attempts and one preceding-build control fail as described above. Across all integration attempts that is 24 passes out of 28 attempts, including repeats and the old-build control; this increment's integration acceptance is explicitly incomplete. No capture or network packet was used for the native handover test; real owned window/audio capture was used only by the existing GUI A/V/video regressions, and standalone endpoint checks use generated tones.

The [validation record](../artifacts/20261022-handover-core-validation.json), [source snapshot](../artifacts/20261022-handover-core-source.zip) and [binary snapshot](../artifacts/20261022-handover-core-binaries.zip) retain these exact outcomes and preceding-build provenance. Snapshot verification checks source/binary/evidence hashes, both new archive contents and the unchanged preceding frozen evidence/archives. Final cleanup is a read-only process audit; only each runner's own children were stopped, and unrelated applications/services were left running.

Then connect this foundation to one persistent `RemoteAvSession` worker and the Qt quality controls. Real local tests must verify one endpoint/audio subscription, both actually painted qualities, switch-window PCM coverage and bounded recovery before claiming continuous UI handover. No VPS deployment is part of this increment.
